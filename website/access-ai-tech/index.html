Project status

What works now, what is being built, and what is planned

The project has three distinct tracks. They share accessibility and safety principles, but they are not equally mature and are not presented as finished products.

Working proof of concept

Streamlit prototype

A browser-based prototype supports typed or Braille-keyboard questions, recorded voice questions, generated spoken answers, and user-triggered still-image tasks for reading, scene description, and object inspection.

Preferences and conversation content remain in the current Streamlit session rather than a durable user account.

In development

Native Android

Android is the primary mobile direction. The current milestone focuses on blind-first onboarding, Android semantics, platform text-to-speech, and local accessibility preferences.

Production AI answers, camera capture, and voice recognition are not connected in that milestone.

Design only

Smart glasses

A vendor-neutral design proposes selective still-frame capture, a phone or controlled backend relay, and concise audio or text output.

No glasses SDK, continuous video service, obstacle avoidance, or navigation capability is implemented.

Technical progress

Concrete implementation facts

01

Accessible setup paths

The Streamlit prototype exposes separate spoken and visual setup routes. Fixed setup prompts use browser speech synthesis and retain equivalent semantic text.

02

Explicit data boundaries

OpenAI-powered requests use a server-side credential. Responses API application-state storage is disabled in the prototype, while provider processing and limited logs may still apply.

03

Regression coverage

Offline application-harness tests cover onboarding routes, accessibility defaults, same-session behavior, missing-key handling, primary controls, and response-storage settings.

Design boundary

Useful assistance without overstating certainty

Visual answers prioritize legible text, practical spatial relationships, nonvisual landmarks, and explicit uncertainty. A single camera image is never represented as proof that a route or environment is safe.

  • No long-lived provider key in a mobile or glasses client.
  • No continuous camera capture in the proposed first glasses milestone.
  • No formal accessibility-conformance claim without physical testing.
